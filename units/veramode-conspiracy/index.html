Guide

Note: The following is NamuWiki community opinion for the KR server. No explicit S/A/B tier rating is stated.

Overall: A "dealporter" (deal + supporter) with excellent utility and survivability while still dealing damage. Two main recommended directions: Xenomancer + Replication Protocol use as a powerful buffer and buff-stripping specialist. Abyss Watcher disrupt enemy skill use, protect allies, and also deal manual damage, i.e. an all-around offense/defense dealporter. The Stun/Faint that Abyss Watcher grants via its passive is so strong that in PVP, where participant count is low and skill dependency is high, he is treated as almost a broken/overpowered character.

Class notes:

  • Xenomancer: support-focused. Veramode's only ally-targeting skill is Replication Protocol, and its penalty disappears on Xenomancer, so it is built around Replication Protocol. The replicated target ends up with ATK and Spell Power +35% total, and Evolution Protocol applies before replication so the Replica also gets Evolution Protocol. Community says just running Xenomancer + Replication Protocol already "pulls its weight".
  • Abyss Watcher: blocks skill use around Veramode. Enemies using a skill within 4 tiles take resistance-ignoring magic damage and become unable to act, blocking the skill itself. Physical and magic skill counters are separate; since he gains Foresight Reaction every 4 enemy actions, in normal PVP roughly 2 of the opponent's characters are stunned nearly every turn. Damage is resistance-ignoring so it is also substantial. Rated as a class of "great majesty" in PVP.
  • Nethermancer: high ceiling for firepower and defense but needs build-up; Dimension Cut can be used every turn. However it needs ramp-up, lacks utility, and the other two classes' advantages are so clear that its recommendation is relatively low.

Weapon / gear:

  • Signature weapon staff, extra stats crit chance and HP. Up to 2-awaken it is focused on flat stats and durability; it shines at high awakenings (TP recovery at 3-awaken, debuff duration -1 at 5-awaken, phase shield at 6-awaken). Needs high awakening to reach full performance.
  • Armor: dumping into Spell Power also boosts tanking; at Legendary use the Chancellor set (motif of his hidden identity), at Hero use the Dark Mage set.
  • Accessories: for dealporter, Fire Crest to strengthen debuffs; for Xenomancer, the Stopwatch to fire Replication Protocol one more time.

Evaluation: Excellent utility and survivability while still dealing damage; a dealporter. To fully use Abyss Watcher's passive he needs to stay relatively close (similar to the Beradin era), but he has many survival tools, a pseudo-revive via Banish, and a shield at high weapon awakening, so performance scales with investment.

Awakening/Manifestation progression: Two tracks per stage: Manifestation and Inner Cube. - Stage 1: Ult+ / Stat+ - Stage 2: Passive+ / Stat+ - Stage 3: Ult+ / Artifact obtained - Stage 4: Passive+ / Artifact+ - Stage 5: Ult+ / Artifact+ - Stage 6: Passive+ / Artifact+

KR Creator Guide

Character review of Veramode of Conspiracy by 하막군의게임튜브 · 2026-08-04

Veramode is where the reviewer starts openly worrying about power creep. He is a mage with more damage reduction than most tanks, enough that early hits routinely land for 1 damage — and he still does enormous damage on top. His verdict is that Veramode is both extremely hard to kill and extremely versatile, and he expects to see him on defence teams constantly, because the four-tile stun aura makes opponents misplay. (26:20)

His investment line is unusually strict: character at 5 manifestations, weapon at ★6. Below that the weapon is close to pointless. (25:17)

Dimensional Field is why he doesn't die

He starts with Dimensional Field — penetration and DoT damage reduction, and a 30% chance on being attacked in combat to cut damage by 50%, scaling to 80% with Crit Rate. The reviewer's point is that Veramode reaches 100% Crit trivially: his classes hand it out, so the 80% version is effectively the default.

Under Abyss Watcher the reduction reaches 110%, and even damage-over-time — Omega's Killing Virus, for instance — is cut by up to 70%. Area attacks avoid the combat-attack clause but still don't land hard. (2:36)

Transcendence Protocol: always take Stimulate

Casting a skill banks Transcendence Protocol equal to the TP spent. At 5 stacks you choose one of three: Awakening (cooldown reduction and Vigor in a 3x3), Shell Reinforcement (shields), or Stimulate.

Take Stimulate. The text says "re-attack or skill use" but it is actually a full extra action, and it adds Focus on the following cast. The reviewer is blunt: almost nothing beats an extra action. (1:04)

Class

Xenomancer applies Suppression Protocol to allies (stats up, Movement up, Vigor, TP at end of action) and Rout Protocol to enemies (-15% across the board) after a skill lands.

The quirk he demonstrates on camera: a skill that buffs himself counts as "target is an ally", so casting Dimensional Cut — which grants Call of the Void — triggers Suppression Protocol and clears his own debuffs, alone. (5:45)

Abyss Watcher is the complicated one, and he says even his guild struggled with it. +40% Crit Rate, -30% damage taken, and a stack of Precognition at game start and whenever an enemy player completes four actions. At end of turn, without one, he gains a random Unknown Conspiracy and spends a Precognition stack.

Unknown Conspiracy comes in two flavours: Mental Interference and Physical Interference. Whichever is active, an enemy casting a matching skill within 4 tiles spends 1 TP, takes 40% of M-ATK ignoring RES, and is stunned — and the skill they tried to cast goes on cooldown without firing. He rates this the single biggest source of opponent mistakes in guild battle. Check which flavour is up and attack with the other damage type. (8:50)

The hard limit worth memorising: Precognition only builds from enemy player actions. NPCs, summons and story mobs do not count at all. (11:24)

Nethermancer stacks Entropy Overload (M-ATK, 4 stacks, unremovable) on every cast and adds a Dimensional Field stack at end of turn — so his defence keeps climbing instead of being stripped. It also reduces Dimensional Cut's cooldown when cast, making a 2-turn skill effectively repeatable. Skipping a cast loses a stack. (11:54)

Gear

Chancellor of Darkness. Accessory: Fire Crest for the Crit Rate, or Necronomicon if you intend to lean on Replication Protocol. (19:36)

Weapon: ★6 or don't bother

Everything about it is built around Phase Shield, which is what keeps him alive. ★3 adds the Magic Battery TP refund on a 3-turn cooldown; ★5 brings Mind Gaze to 3 stacks and Magic Battery to 2 turns; ★6 adds Phase Shield and drops Magic Battery to every turn.

His judgement is unambiguous: the weapon was designed at ★6, and at one copy you are better off with something else. (20:07)

Runes

Crit first. Sun runes in slots 1 and 3 are near-universal on him; the rest is taste, and current builds lean toward Crit-taken reduction rather than the older all-magic setups. (22:10)

Manifestation

2 already gives the full 3 stacks of Dimensional Field — the same as full manifestation — and converts his basic attack into Dark Blast+5 more → with no cooldown or TP cost. 3 and 5 add TP recovery on the ultimate, so you cast it, take the extra action from Stimulate and spend the refunded TP on another skill.

But the value line he names is 5, because that is where the exclusive artifact gains Movement. (23:13)

Artifacts

Fragment of Chaos+1 more → is the one most players will run: Crit damage (which he frames as roughly +40% damage given how easily he reaches 100% Crit), a random debuff on attack, +10% Max HP, and Movement at 5 manifestations. In an SRPG, Movement is the stat that wins.

Meticulous Preparation+10 more → supports the ultimate-then-Dimensional-Cut line. Resonance Stone of Magic+2 more → is good in isolation but loses to Fragment of Chaos on the Movement alone. (24:45)
