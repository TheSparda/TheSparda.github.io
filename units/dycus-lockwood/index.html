Guide

This is subjective NamuWiki community opinion (KR server).

Performance: Base class is Fighter. A Fighter type that can equip an axe instead of a sword. Tier 2 classes are the unique class Berserker and Shakbari's former class Warbone; Tier 3 classes are Unbroken / Warmaster / Genocider. Ultimate is the finally-returned Vacuum Asura Slash. Because he is Fighter-based, the existing Burova items (Burova Sword Guard = sword/shield, Burova Spear Guard = spear/shield) cannot be equipped, and because his defense type is Medium, the Lord of Fierce Strike set based on him also cannot be equipped (a repeat of the Cars situation). Unique passive Dagal's Pride grants attack/crit-damage buffs and control immunity when HP is low, gains Gale on being attacked, and reduces crit chance/crit damage taken when attacked by a 100%-HP enemy. Berserker-like kit that strengthens itself when HP is reduced or when attacked.

Classes:

  • Unbroken: balanced offense/defense with self-heal when debuffed; learns Heavy Slam. Unconditional ATK +25%, damage taken -20%, plus -40% extra in combat attacks; heals 50% of ATK on turn end if debuffed, and once per turn heals 50% of ATK when hit by action control.
  • Warbone: the class Shakbari brought. Like with Shakbari it lags in attack multipliers, and since Dycus has damage reduction on all classes, its self-heal is the only differentiator; but Dycus needs some HP loss to trigger his passive, so it's the lowest-recommended class.
  • Warmaster: learns Bold Offensive. Unconditional ATK up to +50%, damage taken -0.5% per 1% HP lost, and after attacking, until next turn start grants Oath of Victory to self and allies within 2 tiles (DEF/RES up to +50%, counter damage +100%, unremovable). Fits Dycus perfectly since he grows tougher as HP drops, and it spreads an ally-tanking aura as a zone. Since the aura turns on after he attacks, he's ideal as a vanguard to charge in first. Highest raising priority.
  • Genocider: original-game class, learns Soul-Crushing Strike. On attacking, ATK and crit chance up to +40% and crit applies Bleed; damage taken -40% when attacked. Bleed: on turn end, 30% of caster ATK as defense-ignoring damage; if hit by a slash/pierce crit, Excessive Bleed triggers for another 30% ATK defense-ignoring; lasts 3 turns. Simple, strong "hit hard, block hard" class; sacrifices ally buffs and defense vs Warmaster for stronger crit performance, higher ceiling as a dealer. Recommended as a Bleed unit in raids.

Key skills and ultimate:

  • Frenzy Slash: consumes all TP; 3x1 directional AoE for 100% ATK plus 25% ATK per TP consumed, applies Bind, immobilize, 1 turn; +10% physical penetration per 1 TP consumed; cooldown 2 turns. At TP 5, hits for 225% ATK with 50% physical penetration, no split, no counter (AoE).
  • Bold Offensive: TP 1; extra action, clears own debuffs, gain Bold Offensive for 2 turns; cooldown 4 turns. Excellent skill for the vanguard role; only the 4-turn cooldown is a downside.
  • Soul-Crushing Strike: TP 3; 80% ATK to one enemy, knock back up to 3 tiles (+20% ATK per tile knocked), then move to enemy and hit surroundings for 80% ATK; cooldown 2 turns. Up to 220% total damage (80%+60%+80%) at 3-tile knockback; doubles as a mobility skill. Against control-immune targets only 160% lands; recommended to drop in raids since trading TP 3 for 160% is a loss and raid mobility matters less.
  • Battle Stance: TP 1; unremovable Battle Stance buff until next turn start; cooldown 3. A manual attack buff; if you don't attack, you gain Vigor next turn for an extra attack/skill, so it can grant Vigor for just TP 1.
  • Heavy Slam: TP 2; straight-line single target within 2 tiles at +15% crit chance and +15% physical penetration for 140% ATK, applies Weakness Exposure 1 stack; on crit, cooldown -1 and applies another Weakness Exposure stack; cooldown 2. Main damage skill usable every turn if crit is set up; low multiplier for a single-target skill is the drawback, so it leans toward ally synergy. Note (undocumented): when hitting an enemy 1 tile away with nothing in between, he advances toward them, so it counters when firing over obstacles/units but eats counters when nothing is between; for Dycus this can be a merit since he needs to lose HP at the front.
  • ultimate: increases physical penetration then hits enemies within 2 tiles for 170%~180% (?) ATK (base-kit skill lists 185%~210%; guide text states 170%~180%). Simple strong AoE; passive TP-cost reduction means it comes up often. Nicely remade Genesis War 2 staging.

Recommended gear: Unique weapon is first axe-type unique weapon; extra stats crit chance and HP, granting Hot Blade and Sandstorm. Excellent weapon that at high awakening adds skill cooldown reduction; best in 1-vs-many; but only Dycus can equip axes so versatility is worst. Armor: since skills and the unique weapon have physical penetration, Ilgidangcheon is a good pick; if going crit-dealer Genocider, the Crow set is also viable. Accessory: Ancient Hero's Ring is best; for crit-dealer Genocider, Arrowhead is an option, and Amber if crit damage is sufficient.

Evaluation: A simple, strong melee dealer. Some classes give strong passives when moving first among allies, and Bold Offensive gives good mobility, so a vanguard suits him. However, spec output has various conditions (moving first, HP loss, stacking), so he's surprisingly hard to run mindlessly. Early on his Warmaster class was expected as a Siren accessory, but the cumbersome conditions became a problem; as of 26.01 he's used more in PVE than PVP. Utility is somewhat low, but raw stats are good, armed with an AoE that has no conditional damage reduction, and his ultimate is easy to use, so he can shine in story battles. In raids, unless enemy-attribute is banned, he's worth using as a Weakness Exposure + Bleed unit. Use him split across parties 1 and 2 alongside Shakbari.

No S/A/B tier letter rating is present in the text.

KR Creator Guide

Character review of Dycus Lockwood by 하막군의게임튜브 · 2026-08-04

The reviewer opens with the joke that the whole review is one word — don't pull — then spends twenty minutes explaining why, and he is unusually direct about it. Dycus was promised almost from launch and arrived near the second anniversary; his own copy is 4★ with no exclusive weapon, so the weapon section is read off the banner screen and the practical detail comes from a guildmate running him fully built. (0:01, 0:34, 1:06, 1:38)

His actual recommendation: pull him if he's your favourite character, if you're a completionist, or if money isn't a consideration. Otherwise skip and save for Cyrano Bernstein, who is next. The comparison he keeps returning to is Shakvari of War — same attribute, same shape, and Shakvari was obsoleted quickly. (2:09, 2:39, 21:38, 22:08)

Pride of Dagal

Max HP up to +40%. At the start of the allied turn, below a health threshold — 75% at max awakening, and simply "not full" at full manifestation — he gains Pride of Dagal and 1 extra TP. The threshold is the awkward part at lower investment: 75% means taking a real beating first.

Pride of Dagal itself is large: attack and critical damage up to +60%, plus immunity to movement-hindering and position-control effects (stun and cooldown control join at full manifestation). Being attacked also grants Gust, and each Gust stack takes 1 TP off Vacuum Asura Slash.

There's a clause he flags as dead weight: the crit-taken reduction only applies against an attacker at 100% HP. Heidelun walks in, casts Entropy Ghost, and is no longer at full health — so against him the whole line does nothing. (3:09, 3:39, 4:10, 4:41)

The attack bonuses pile up all over this kit, and he's careful not to be impressed by that on its own. The damage reduction is scattered around too, and the durability still isn't especially good. His word for the character is "awkward". (4:41, 5:11)

Class: Genocider if you want him to hit

Warmaster is +50~60% ATK, damage reduction scaling with missing health, and after attacking an enemy an aura until the start of his next turn giving himself and allies within 2 tiles Oath of Victory — defence and +100% counterattack damage.

He calls that "completely made for Siren", since Commander Siren Woodville already scales counterattack damage. But two problems: the aura only appears after he attacks something, so he can't simply stand next to her, and it expires at the start of his next turn, so she has to act before he does. (5:11, 5:43, 6:15, 6:45)

Genocider is the one he'd take. Attack and Crit Rate up when attacking, -40% damage taken when attacked, and Bleed on a critical hit — an upgraded version of Shakvari of War's Slasher.

The decisive detail: the passive hands out up to +60% critical damage, and Genocider is the only class in the tree that gives Crit Rate at all. If you're building him to deal damage there is no real choice. (6:45, 7:15, 7:48)

Gear

He'd split One Against a Thousand two-and-two with a second set rather than running the full four.

The accessory is where he spends the most thought. The passive gives up to +60% critical damage, but Crit Rate appears in exactly one place in the whole kit — Genocider's 40% — on top of a base 8%. So Balanced Blade Necklace, which trades critical damage for +30% Crit Rate, comes out ahead even after the trade. Black Crow is the other reasonable direction, precisely because his critical damage scaling is so high. (11:24, 11:54, 12:26, 12:57)

The weapon isn't optional

Burova Optima stacks Hot Blade by the number of enemies damaged — so hitting two enemies gives two stacks, and so does hitting one enemy twice, which his multi-hit skills do by themselves. Four stacks is +40% physical penetration. Reaching max stacks, or landing a killing blow, grants Sandstorm: -10% damage taken per stack, and at end of turn 100% of ATK to everything in one ring plus a cooldown reduction equal to the number of enemies that ring hit.

He rates the ring highly — a free 100% hit is not a normal basic attack — and notes it feeds Bold Offensive back into availability, which is the fix for that skill's cooldown. Both effects are unremovable and hold as long as he keeps attacking; two turns without an attack and the stacks are gone.

One caveat from his guild's raid players, which he flags as untested by him: "lethal damage" often doesn't register in raid content, so the kill-triggered half of the stacking may not work where you'd most want it. His conclusion: if you're going to play Dycus, the weapon is mandatory. (13:28, 13:59, 14:29, 14:59, 15:32, 16:02, 16:34)

Runes

Chaos in slot 1 as standard, and he notes Sun in slot 2 has become popular — it's percentage attack, which will matter more when the authority level cap rises to 60. Otherwise all Chaos is fine, or Chaos/Ultimate/Moon if you want health. He observes that players increasingly ignore set bonuses and just maximise the flat lines. (16:34, 17:04, 17:34, 18:04)

Manifestation

The health threshold for Pride of Dagal loosens each stage — 80%, then 90%, then simply "not at full health" — and the Gust chance climbs to guaranteed. At full manifestation Max HP is +40% and critical damage +60%, with stun and cooldown-control immunity added.

He points out a practical opener: since he can equip greatswords, an Asura lets him start a turn already holding Pride of Dagal, or you park Esmeralda beside him for the same effect.

His guildmate's fully manifested copy lands around 14,000 HP with roughly 50% critical damage taken reduction — durable enough, he says, without being remarkable. (19:06, 19:37, 20:07, 20:37)

Artifacts

Warrior's Essence+10 more → is attack and physical penetration. Crest of Chains+2 more → ticks up as he keeps attacking. Black Iron Heart reduces magic damage taken and returns 1 TP when he's hit by a magic attack.

He finds all three unremarkable and picks Crest of Chains+2 more → by default, with Warrior's Essence+10 more → as the alternative. (20:37, 21:07)

Verdict

A simple, strong character in a game that has moved past simple and strong. He sees a narrow use — a two- or three-manifestation copy following Commander Siren Woodville around and feeding her counterattack damage, a second run at the old iron-body build — and beyond that not much. Six free copies get you to 6★, which is enough for that job.

His closing advice is unambiguous: skip this one and put the money into Cyrano Bernstein. (21:38, 22:08, 22:38)
