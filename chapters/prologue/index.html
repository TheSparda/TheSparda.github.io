Prologue

The Battle of Gratias

EY 1202 — the Geysir Empire shatters the Pandragon Kingdom in a single day.

The Origin of the War

The conflict that destroyed Pandragon did not start with Pandragon. It started with grain. Geysir is a mountain nation with a chronic, structural food shortage — it depended heavily on grain imports from the free-trading state of Cyrups to feed its army and population. When Cyrups raised its prices, Geysir used the economic blow as a pretext to invade. Cyrups served as a geographic gateway to Pandragon, and Pandragon could not afford to watch its buffer state fall — so it mobilized the Silver Arrow alliance and marched to defend it. That march led to the Battle of Gratias.

The chain of cause is important: a grain price dispute in a third country pulled Pandragon into a war it did not start, against an enemy it had not fully prepared to fight at the strategic level. The Silver Arrow alliance outnumbered Geysir's forces on the field — but not the Black Prince's mind.

The Fall of Pandragon

On the Gratias Plain, the Geysir Empire shatters the Pandragon Kingdom. The royal house is gutted in a day. The people lost there define the survivors' wounds.

King Ashur Pandragon XVII — the Pandragon monarch and pilot of the royal Machina Arondite, killed at Gratias by the Black Prince. His Arondite and the royal sword Barisada that keys it become the buried inheritance that eventually reaches Lacid through Crow.

Akron Pandragon and Lint Pandragon — princes of the house, both killed; Lint had served as a Silver Arrow advisor. Iyolin and Lacid's brothers, gone in an afternoon.

Ice Pandragon — the greatest swordsman the kingdom ever produced. He does not die; he is defeated by the Black Prince and flees, vanishing from history. The specific moment that broke the Pandragon line: witnessing his father Ashur fall, Ice Pandragon lost his composure, abandoned tactical discipline, and charged the Black Prince directly in grief and rage. The Black Prince called him "not as great as the rumour, and worst as a commander" — the arrogant solo charge had broken Pandragon's tactical advantage and handed the enemy the opening it needed. Ice knew it. He fled carrying that knowledge.

On the Imperial side, the host at Gratias is the Empire's full elite: the Seven Knights — Vincent, Kashutar, Hanzo, Vishnu, Karls, Agassi — and the desert overlord Kasim, all under the Black Prince. Against them the Silver Arrow line of Ashur, Akron, Lint, Ice, and veterans including Zeke Stafford, Durant Rembrandt, Dycus Rockwood, and Astania's Erich is simply outmatched — not in numbers, but in command. The Black Prince devised a diagonal formation specifically to break Pandragon's standard defensive arrangement, exploiting the gap Ice's suicidal charge tore open.

Two royal children survive the massacre: Iyolin and the young Lacid.
